# Candidates and tournament record — third entry

Six new verse candidates were written against the pattern that wins this
board: short lines whose breaks slow the reading, an intimate concrete
scene with a family figure, cosmic or deep-time scale arriving through
that small scene, and a landing in stillness. Together with the standing
entry's text ("Porch swing", 241 code points) as the comparison champion,
the seven texts played a full round-robin: every pair judged twice, once
in each presentation order, with the competition's published
relaxation-judge brief (relaxes first; calm shown, not instructed; craft
and pacing; the image that keeps growing after the last line; freshness).
Margins are 1–5 per game.

## The candidates

**C1 — Stairs (247 cp)**
Father carries you up the stairs, one slow step, then one. Your head on
his shoulder, your shoes still on. The house is dark and warm. Above the
roof, the moon is older than the hill it climbs, and it will climb it
still when the stairs are quiet.

**C2 — Clock (235 cp)**
Grandfather winds the clock in the hall, the key turning slow. It has
ticked through every winter of his life. He sets the hands the way his
father taught him, and the tick goes on, patient as rain on a roof, long
after his hands rest.

**C3 — Seedlings (240 cp)** — winner
At dusk your grandmother waters the seedlings, cup by cup. The soil
drinks slow. Above her, the first stars open like small white flowers,
older than any garden, and she hums to the rows she will not see grown,
content to have watered them.

**C4 — Mending (229 cp)**
Your mother sews by the window, needle in, needle out, mending the coat
you tore. Rain threads the glass. The sea that made the rain is older
than the town, and her stitches hold, small and even, while you sleep in
the next room.

**C5 — Stove (235 cp)**
Grandfather feeds the stove one stick at a time. The kettle warms slow.
Outside, snow falls on fields his grandfather ploughed, and the fire he
tends is older than the house, kept alive by hands like his, burning
gentle into the night.

**C6 — Fireflies (213 cp)**
You hold the jar of fireflies up to the dark field. One by one they lift,
blinking, unhurried, joining the low stars that burned before there were
fields. Your hands are empty now, and the whole field is blinking.

## Results (winner, margins in the two presentation orders)

- C3 beat Porch swing 2 and 1 — the deciding match. The stars opening
  "like small white flowers" is the freshest image in the field, and the
  close — humming to rows she will not see grown, content to have
  watered them — keeps growing after the last line: her care outlasts
  her, under stars older than any garden.
- C3 beat C1 (2, 1), C2 (2, 2), C4 (3, 2), C5 (2, 2), C6 (1, 2) — 6–0.
- Porch swing beat C1 (2, 2), C2 (2, 1), C4 (2, 2), C5 (2, 1), C6 (1, 2)
  — 5–1. Its "Pod, peas, hush" repetition remains the strongest pure
  sound-slowing device; it lost only on freshness and after-image.
- C1 beat C2 (1, 2), C4 (2, 2), C5 (2, 1), C6 (2, 1) — 4–2.
- C2 beat C4 (1, 2) and C5 (1, 1), split with C6 (1 each way).
- C6 beat C4 (2, 2) and C5 (1, 2). C5 beat C4 (1, 1).

Final records: C3 6–0, Porch swing 5–1, C1 4–2, C2 and C6 mid-field,
C5 1 win, C4 0. C3 is the only text that beat the champion in both
orders, by a combined margin of 3, and it holds the best record — so
C3's text is the third entry.
